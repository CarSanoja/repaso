# Probe ablation — September 12, 2026

The options-only answerability probe costs one model call for every generated item and returns a
signal that cannot discard anything: `combine` records it on the verdict and leaves acceptance to
the critic. The protocol in [`evaluation/README.md`](../../evaluation/README.md) asks whether that
call is worth making. It was run on September 12, 2026 in us-east-1. Numbers below come from
[`probe-ablation-2026-09-12.json`](probe-ablation-2026-09-12.json), the row capture in
[`evaluation/ablation_observations.csv`](../../evaluation/ablation_observations.csv) and the
transcript in `evaluation/ablation_transcript.jsonl`, which keeps every critic verdict and every
probe reply.

## These are construction labels, not teacher labels

The protocol says a teacher labels validity and option clues independently before seeing any model
output. No teacher has reviewed these items, so that step did not happen and its result is not
reported here.

What replaces it is weaker and is named as such everywhere in the artifacts: the defects were
planted by the harness, so they are known by construction. An item labelled `construction_valid`
carries **no planted defect**. That is not the claim that a teacher read it and called it sound —
nobody outside the construction has read it. Every retention and rejection figure below is about
planted defects and about nothing else, and the teacher-labelled version of this result does not
exist. The capture file says so in its own columns: `independent_reviewer` is `none` on all 270
rows, `label_source` is `construction`, and the two label columns are named `construction_valid`
and `construction_option_clue` rather than the `teacher_*` names the empty template carried, so
that no reader can mistake one for the other.

## The item set

Thirty multiple-choice items were generated by `us.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6` from the
fourth-grade equivalent-fractions notebook page the project already owns
(`demo/cuaderno-fracciones.png`), against the equivalence and comparison competencies of the
bundled taxonomy. Eighteen were left exactly as generated. Twelve were planted:

| planted defect | items | what was done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wrong_key` | 4 | the key was moved onto an option that is not correct |
| `implausible_distractors` | 4 | every distractor replaced by something no child would weigh |
| `option_cue` | 4 | the key extended into the longest, most textbook-sounding option |

Eight of the twelve — the distractor and cue plants — carry a planted option clue: the key can be
picked from the option list alone. Option order was permuted with seeds 1, 2 and 3, giving 90
decisions per arm over 30 items. The frozen set keeps both the generated text and the planted text
for every item, so the labels can be audited without trusting the planter.

## The arms

One critic call per item and permutation is **shared** by the three arms, so whatever separates
them is the probe and not critic sampling. The critic ran on `us.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6`, both
probes on `us.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0`; the historical prompt and its veto are reconstructed from the
commit that introduced them.

| measure (denominator) | critic only | + historical forced-choice veto | + advisory probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| unplanted retained (54 decisions) | 20 — 37.0% [25.4–50.4] | 7 — 13.0% [6.4–24.4] | 20 — 37.0% [25.4–50.4] |
| planted rejected (36 decisions) | 34 — 94.4% [81.9–98.5] | 36 — 100% [90.4–100] | 34 — 94.4% [81.9–98.5] |
| finalized key correct (of accepted) | 22/22 — 100% [85.1–100] | 7/7 — 100% [64.6–100] | 22/22 — 100% [85.1–100] |
| items accepted | 22 | 7 | 22 |
| model calls | 90 | 180 | 180 |
| input / output tokens | 149,817 / 15,670 | 207,041 / 17,208 | 198,878 / 17,034 |
| spend | $0.6845 | $0.6867 | $0.6864 |
| measured latency per decision | 3,352 ms | 3,847 ms | 3,849 ms |
| provider or schema errors | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Intervals are Wilson 95%. Rejection by planted defect is in the JSON artifact; each of those rates
is over 12 decisions, which is four items seen three times.

Two accounting notes. The shared critic call is charged to all three arms, so the arm column is
what that arm would have spent, not three separate bills: the run's real ledger total is $0.9191,
of which $0.6845 is the shared critic, $0.2305 a second critic pass described below, and $0.0041
both probe layers together. "Latency per decision" is the sum of the measured latencies of the
calls that arm makes, which is what a family waits through because the ingest graph validates items
one after another; the whole collection took 492 seconds of wall clock.

The measurement as a whole cost $1.2713 over 308 live calls, priced by the dated table from the
usage the provider reported: 8 calls and $0.3522 to generate the items, 300 calls and $0.9191 to
run the arms.

## The advisory probe changed no decision, and its signal does not track the planted clue

It cannot change a decision: `combine` only records. Ninety decisions confirm that, with no errors.
So the question is whether the signal it records is worth reading, and on this set it is not.

The probe is told to look for a much longer option or implausible distractors and to copy the
option a clue favours, or else answer UNKNOWN. On the 24 decisions where exactly such a clue was
planted it answered UNKNOWN 23 times. It matched the key on 1 of 24 clued decisions (4.2%
[0.7–20.2]) and on 8 of 66 unclued ones (12.1% [6.3–22.1]) — it fires slightly *less* often where
the clue is real. By planted defect it matched the key on 0 of 12 implausible-distractor decisions,
1 of 12 cue decisions, 0 of 12 wrong-key decisions and 8 of 54 unplanted ones; eight of its nine
hits were a single digit. A signal this loosely coupled to what it was built to detect cannot be
read as evidence about an item.

The historical forced-choice probe, which sees the stem, is a different instrument with the same
problem. It matched the key on 57 of 90 decisions: 16 of 24 clued (66.7% [46.7–82.0]) and 41 of 66
unclued (62.1% [50.1–72.9]). It mostly measures that a capable model can answer fourth-grade
fraction questions, which the protocol says is not a defect, and its veto acts on that. The veto
flipped 15 keep decisions to drop: 13 were unplanted items and 2 were cue plants the critic had
kept. That is the whole of its incremental catch — two planted decisions bought with thirteen
unplanted ones — and it is why retention falls from 37.0% to 13.0% while planted rejection rises
from 94.4% to 100%.

## Why retention is low in every arm

The critic rejected 34 of the 54 unplanted decisions. The flaws it named there were `ambiguous`
(16), `wrong_competency` (12), `answerable_without_material` (12) and `implausible_distractors`
(5). Reading its notes, most of those objections are about grounding rather than about arithmetic:
items tagged to the comparison competency that actually practise equivalence, and items that ask a
child to simplify 6/10 or to compare 2/3 with 5/8 when the notebook page only ever shows
multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number. A teacher might well agree with the
critic on those. Nobody has asked one, so this artifact reports the rejections and makes no claim
about who is right. It is the clearest reason why "unplanted retention" must not be read as
"valid-item retention".

Ten of the eighteen unplanted items were rejected under all three option orders, five were kept
under all three, and three flipped with order (retention by permutation: 8/18, 6/18, 6/18). Two of
the twelve planted items — both cue plants — were kept under one order and rejected under the other
two.

## How much of this is noise

The critic was asked a second time about every item under the first permutation, same prompt, same
order: 29 of 30 decisions came back the same (96.7% [83.3–99.4]). One item in thirty flips on a
repeat with nothing changed. The advisory arm differs from the critic-only arm by zero decisions,
which is smaller than that.

## Failures and limits

- No teacher labels. Everything above is about planted defects.
- Thirty items from one page, one subject, one grade, one language, one model snapshot, one day.
  Four items per planted defect type; each per-defect rate is over 12 decisions.
- The keys of the eighteen unplanted items were not independently verified. If the generator wrote
  a wrong key into one of them, this run counts a correct rejection as a retention loss.
- "Finalized key correctness" is construction-limited: it says the accepted item's key is not a
  key that was deliberately corrupted. No arm rewrites keys, so this measure can only fall when an
  arm accepts a `wrong_key` plant, and none did.
- The three arms share one critic call. That isolates the probe, and it means the arms are not
  three independent runs.
- Item generation itself is a live call and is not reproducible; that is why the set is frozen in
  the repository rather than regenerated.

## Recommendation

**Disable the options-only advisory probe in the ingest path.** On this set it changed no decision,
by construction it cannot, and the signal it records did not track the planted clue it exists to
find — it stayed silent on 23 of the 24 decisions where the clue was real. Keep the agent and this
harness for evaluation, where the same call can be made deliberately against a labelled set.

What disabling saves, per material a family sends in: the ingest graph validates six generated
items, so six calls, 3,270 input and 90 output tokens, about 3.0 seconds of the wait before a
family's items are ready, and $0.00013. The money is not the argument — at a thousand materials it
is thirteen cents. The argument is that the call currently buys nothing that can be read.

**Do not restore the historical veto.** It reached 100% rejection of planted defects by discarding
13 of 54 unplanted decisions, and what it mostly detects is that a capable model can answer a fair
fourth-grade question.

**What would change this answer**: teacher labels on this same frozen set, which would say whether
the critic's 34 unplanted rejections were right and whether the probe's silence cost anything a
teacher can see. Until then the ablation answers the cost question and leaves the quality question
open.
