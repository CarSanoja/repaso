# Agents for Humans: The tests that made Repaso change its product claims

Repaso's strongest review findings came from cases where a plausible feature did not do what its description suggested. A button selected a teacher note, but the system sent only an acknowledgment. A rejected human review did not become an incorrect learning outcome. A family deletion left material bytes and generated items behind. Each required a change in behavior and a check of the resulting effects.

One finding changed an evaluation idea. The old answerability probe saw a math question and its options, was forced to guess and could reject the item after one correct answer. That does not establish a defective exercise. Prior knowledge may be exactly what practice is meant to use, and a guess can be right by chance.

The new probe sees only the options and may abstain. Its result is advisory. It cannot veto an otherwise valid exercise simply by guessing correctly. The proposed ablation compares critic-only review, the old forced-choice veto and options-only advice on the same teacher-labeled items, including permutations. Valid-item retention and defective-item rejection need separate denominators. No improvement in educational quality is claimed before that experiment is completed.

The grading evaluation has a similar boundary. A valid JSON response is useful technical evidence; it is not evidence that a child was graded correctly. A model's 0.85 confidence is not a calibrated probability. Repaso therefore includes 60 synthetic answer examples, split by question group into development and held-out sets, and a blind teacher-review file without author labels or model predictions.

Those independent labels are blank today. The scoring tool refuses to turn proposed author labels or incomplete reviews into independent agreement. It reports coverage, reviewed sample counts, false automatic-correct decisions, confidence bands and uncertainty intervals when valid observations exist. Empty data remains empty; it does not become perfect accuracy.

The evidence register also separates four levels. Ordinary tests verify software behavior. A 14-day authored simulation exercises 30 synthetic students and 420 local sessions, with 1,037 responses and all nine scenario gates satisfied. A separate actual Textract call recognized a new printed Spanish fractions page. Live Bedrock quality and actual family outcomes remain unmeasured because inference access and human review are still pending.

This distinction affects the product promise. The candidate accepts printed fourth-grade math material and a one-page PDF. Voice and Spanish handwriting are excluded. Deletion covers active state and material versions, while consent explains operational-log, backup and Telegram retention. A small shared-section threshold is not described as formal anonymity.

The family pilot is designed to expose added work as well as savings. It records minutes spent preparing, accompanying, reviewing and fixing mistakes, along with operator assistance and incomplete sessions. A family choosing to use the routine tomorrow would be useful evidence; a learning-effect claim would require a different study.

These corrections make the project easier to judge. The goal is to make the product, demonstration and evidence describe the same system—even when the honest result is that a promising idea still needs a real test.
